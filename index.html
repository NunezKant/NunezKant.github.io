Most people who understand the brain can't build AI systems. Most AI scientists don't understand the brain. I sit at the intersection — trained as a Biomedical Engineer, holding a PhD in Systems & Computational Neuroscience, and spending the last five years at HHMI Janelia Research Campus pushing the limits of what ML can reveal about neural computation.

At Janelia, I work with recordings from populations of up to 50,000 neurons, using machine learning and mathematical theory to understand how the brain encodes information, learns, and makes decisions. My 2025 Nature Communications paper introduced a simplified minimodel of visual cortical neurons — a step toward understanding the computational primitives the brain uses to process the world.

The skills I've built — Bayesian and causal inference, deep learning on high-dimensional biosignals, scalable ML pipelines — map directly onto the challenges in healthcare AI, drug discovery, and clinical data science. I'm now actively exploring opportunities in industry, with a focus on CNS/neuroscience, health AI, and computational science roles in Montreal and Barcelona.
